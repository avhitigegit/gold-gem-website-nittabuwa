    Rough Story

    From Earth to Brilliance

    Every Gold Gem stone begins as rough gravel in the river valleys of Sri Lanka. This is how we bring it to you.

    1. 01

      Finding the Illam

      Our miners search the ancient gem gravel layer — the illam — buried beneath paddy fields and riverbeds around Ratnapura and Elahera.

    2. 02

      Traditional Pit Mining

      Small, licensed pits are dug by hand and supported with timber, the same low-impact method used in Sri Lanka for over 2,000 years.

    3. 03

      Washing the Gravel

      Gravel is washed in woven baskets in flowing water. Heavy gem crystals sink and are picked out by experienced eyes.

    4. 04

      Sorting the Rough

      Rough stones are graded by colour, clarity and size. Only crystals with real potential move on to the cutting table.

    5. 05

      Cutting & Polishing

      Our lapidaries plan each cut to unlock maximum colour and brilliance while respecting the weight of the rough.

    6. 06

      Certified & Set

      Stones are tested, certified on request, and either offered loose or hand-set into gold and silver jewelry.

    Gem Journal

    Know Your Gems

    Short reads on Ceylon gems, jewelry and why the source matters.

    Ceylon Gems

    What Makes Ceylon Gems So Valuable?

    Sri Lanka has been called Ratna Dweepa — the Island of Gems — for more than two thousand years. Its gem gravels formed from ancient metamorphic rock, producing sapphires with an exceptional, luminous brightness. Ceylon blue sapphires are known for a lighter, livelier cornflower tone that sparkles even in low light, while padparadscha is found in meaningful quantities almost nowhere else on earth.

    Value comes from rarity, colour, clarity and origin. Many Ceylon stones are clean enough to need little or no treatment, and an unheated stone with a strong colour commands a premium worldwide. Famous jewels, including royal engagement rings, carry Ceylon sapphires — so a "Ceylon origin" note on a lab report adds both prestige and long-term value to your gem.

    Gem Facts

    Rocks, Minerals & Gems — The Difference

    A rock is a mix of minerals, like granite. A mineral is a single natural substance with a fixed chemistry and crystal structure. A gem is a mineral rare, beautiful and durable enough to be cut and worn.

    Here's a surprising fact: sapphire and ruby are the same mineral — corundum, a crystal of aluminium oxide. Tiny traces of chromium turn it red (ruby); iron and titanium turn it blue. Every other colour is still called sapphire.

    Corundum scores 9 on the Mohs hardness scale, second only to diamond, which is why sapphires survive everyday wear in rings. Star stones get their glow from fine needle-like rutile inclusions that reflect light into a six-rayed star — a flaw that becomes magic.

    Buying Guide

    Why Buy Gems From Sri Lanka?

    Buying in Sri Lanka puts you at the heart of one of the world's oldest gem trades. The island produces an astonishing variety — sapphires in every colour, spinel, alexandrite, cat's eye, garnet, moonstone and more — all from a relatively small area.

    The government regulates mining and export through the National Gem & Jewellery Authority, and gem testing is widely available, giving buyers real confidence. Mining here is still largely small-scale and hand-dug, which keeps the environmental footprint low and supports local village communities.

    Most of all, you get choice and value. Buying where the stones are mined means wider selection, fairer prices, and the story of a gem that travelled from a Sri Lankan riverbed straight into your jewelry.
